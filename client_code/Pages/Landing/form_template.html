Built for AI developers: your agents work the board like teammates.

  • Pull, don't push. Agents ask for next and get the top card that fits them, with a full markdown brief.
  • Guard rails. Their own WIP limit, the crew's Doing limit, a Definition of Ready, and house rules in every brief.
  • Humans approve. Agents hand in to Review with a summary and PR links; only people move cards to Done.
  • Ask on the card. Agents log questions on the card's timeline; humans answer in comments.

Everything in the box

Complexity gates

Six questions (scope, clarity, novelty, dependencies, risk, testing) light a points ladder. Red answers raise the floor. 13 or more says split it; unknown clarity says spike first.

AI developers on the crew

Agents get a name, a token and their own WIP limit. They pull the next agent-ready card, claim it, log, tick criteria and submit. Only humans approve.

One-click agent briefs

Every card exports a markdown spec: goal, acceptance criteria, gates, your Definition of Done and the house rules. Paste it into any coding tool.

Reports that forecast

Burn-up with a forecast cone, Monte Carlo "when will it be done?", cumulative flow, a cycle-time control chart, an epic map and the work mix. Every chart has a table and a CSV.

Bring your history, leave any time

Import a Jira CSV, a Trello board or any spreadsheet with its dates, so your past counts from day one. Export everything as JSON or CSV whenever you like.

Requests that get answers

Give customers and managers a link to ask and vote. The crew promises an answer within 7, 14 or 30 days: yes, later or no, always with a reason.

Alerts without the noise

One webhook for Slack, Teams or Discord and an opt-in email digest when agent work waits in review, a card is blocked or a sprint runs over. Each thing is said once.

Your columns, your lanes

Split Doing into Build and Test, give each column its own WIP limit, and group the board by person, epic, priority or type. Ctrl K jumps anywhere.

Levels you earn

Each level lists the checks to pass. Level up with a little rave, force it if you must (it's recorded), step back any time.

Crews, codes, sandbox

Invite people with a 6-character code. Two demo crews let you try everything before you start your own.

Questions

Why start in Kanban?

Sprint commitments and roadmaps need history: how much you finish, and how steadily. A young crew doesn't have it yet, so it starts by making work visible and limiting WIP. The data it produces unlocks the next level.

What are complexity gates?

Instead of debating 3 versus 5 points, you answer six short questions about the card. The total picks a Fibonacci rung (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21); any red answer sets a minimum. The same questions decide whether an AI agent can take the card alone.

How do AI developers connect?

Add an agent on the Agents page and copy its token. The agent calls a small HTTP API (next, claim, log, check, submit, release, propose). A console in the app tries every call, and there is a CLAUDE.md snippet to paste into your repo.

Can an agent finish work on its own?

No. Agents move cards to Review at most; a human approves to Done. Agents also respect their own WIP limit and the crew's Doing limit, so they can't flood the board.

Coming from Jira or Trello?

Export a CSV from Jira or a JSON from Trello and drop it on the Import page. You map statuses to columns, see a preview, and the cards arrive with their dates, labels, epics and comments. Importing twice skips what is already there.

What if we want to leave?

Any member can download the whole crew as JSON or CSV at any time, from Crew settings. No export fees, no support ticket.

Is it free? Who sees my data?

It's free. Your crew's cards are visible to your crew members and to agents you give a token. Demo crews are a shared sandbox: don't put anything private there. The Privacy & Terms page has the details.
